## Shared material

- `فِعْلُ الْأَمْرِ` expresses a request for the addressee to perform an action.
- It is always built (`مَبْنِيٌّ`).
- The second-person masculine/feminine singular and plural forms and their subject markers.
- Formation from the present verb by removing the present-tense prefix and adding `هَمْزَةُ الْوَصْلِ` when the resulting initial consonant is quiescent.
- The initial vowel is ḍammah when `عَيْنُ الْمُضَارِعِ` has ḍammah and kasrah when it has fatḥah or kasrah.

## Material only or more explicit in the 80-page sharh

- The dual form `اِذْهَبَا` and all five addressee forms together.
- Four construction signs: sukūn, deletion of nūn, deletion of a weak letter, and fatḥah with emphatic nūn.
- Complete iʿrāb for `اِذْهَبْ`, `اِذْهَبَا`, `اِذْهَبِي`, `اِذْهَبُوا`, and `اِذْهَبْنَ`.
- A four-step derivation scheme and the four present prefixes `أ، ت، ي، ن`.

## Material only or more explicit in the 62-page sharh

- The three verb classes are named before the imperative explanation.
- The bare-alif spelling rule: the triliteral imperative has `هَمْزَةُ وَصْلٍ`, so no hamzah head is written above or below its initial alif.
- Twelve distinct present/imperative pairs across the two sources.
- The rule that a final sukūn is changed to kasrah before a following definite article, with `اِشْرَبِ الْقَهْوَةَ` and `اِفْهَمِ الدَّرْسَ`.
- The contrast `اُخْرُجْ وَالْعَبْ`, where no kasrah is needed because the following wāw is not quiescent.
- The source forms of `اِذْهَبُوا` and `اِذْهَبِي` from `تَذْهَبُونَ` and `تَذْهَبِينَ`.
- The definition of `عَيْنُ الْفِعْلِ` as the second root letter in the past form.
